### Pre-launch review: 6 defects confirmed of 30 raised

Registered before any panel data existed. The blocking one: progress was being
read from the BDDL goal conjunction, which exposes only the `place` half of
each subgoal. §3.1.1 defines p(t) over the frozen `GoalAutomaton`, which also
has `pick_up` EVENT milestones - the class V7.7 actually measured. Consequences
were concrete: the §13.1 per-class distribution could not be produced, it was
not recoverable post hoc (no body positions stored), and `failure_phase` over
place-only milestones gave the 1-subgoal rungs exactly one reachable phase,
making `chain1`/`chain1b` ineligible for `PASS_MIN_FAILURE_PHASES=2` before a
single episode ran. Also fixed: W/H_max were frozen from one pooled q90 across
tasks and milestone positions (now per class, frozen scalar = max over
classes); and the manifest called the checkpoint "stock pi0.5, no fine-tuning"
while silently loading a LIBERO fine-tune at an unpinned Hub revision.

The skeptic pass corrected the reviewers where they overreached: pooling
H_max is conservative rather than a repeat of the V7.7 truncation error
(pick->place is a sub-interval of place->place), and W never relocates anchors
- §3.1.1 Tier 1 places tau at `t_prog` and W only classifies stalled/not.

### Two further defects the data itself exposed

1. **Negative intervals.** The chain goals are unordered conjunctions and
   pi0.5 solves them out of order - on `chain2b`, 6/10 episodes took cream
   cheese before tomato sauce. Differencing completion steps by subgoal index
   gave `chain2b|2|pick_up cream_cheese_1` a q90 of **-178.5**. Intervals are
   now gaps between successive achievements in time. Framework §3.1.1 amended:
   p(t) is the ordered prefix for order-enforcing goals and the count of
   achieved milestones for unordered conjunctions.
2. **Unseeded policy sampling.** pi0.5 samples its action chunk; the same seed
   gave 138 and 134 terminal steps on two runs. Episodes now seed torch, numpy
   and CUDA. The first panel is archived as
   `SUPERSEDED_*_negative_intervals_unseeded/` with its own note; its
   per-episode records are kept, its derived block is not citable.

### The failure frontier, which is what Action 2 will anchor on

| task | failure signature | count |
|---|---|---|
| `chain1b` | picked the sauce, **could not place it** | 16/18 |
| `chain1b` | could not pick the sauce at all | 2/18 |
| `chain2b` | sauce done, **stuck on `pick_up cream_cheese`** | 5/10 |
| `chain2b` | could not pick the sauce | 2/10 |
| `chain2b` | nothing achieved | 2/10 |
| `chain2b` | picked sauce, placed nothing | 1/10 |

This corrects a reading I gave on the screen data. The dominant failure is
**not** instruction-following. `chain1b`'s 16/18 modal failure is a placement
failure *after* a successful grasp - a control/dynamics failure, which is the
class the branch's premise is about. Instruction-following shows up as the
~3x pick slowdown (median 205 vs 70 steps for the same object as second
object), and it dominates `chain2b`'s cream-cheese stall, but it is not the
whole frontier. The two selected tasks therefore fail in two different
milestone classes, which is exactly what §13.1's phase criterion is for.

One structural fact for Action 2's anchor extractor: `chain2b` solved out of
order in **20/30** confirmation episodes. Anchors on that task cannot assume a
canonical milestone sequence, and the §3.1.1 amendment (p(t) = count of
achieved milestones for unordered conjunctions) is load-bearing, not cosmetic.
