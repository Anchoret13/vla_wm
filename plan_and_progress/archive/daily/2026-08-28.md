## Goal (restated, per CLAUDE.md)

Deploy a frozen VLA on a task it has a **low** success rate on, collect **real
trajectories during deployment**, and train a **latent-space** world model that
predicts future state — action-conditioned, **no reconstruction** — to make the
VLA better at deployment.

Framework §5.2: `z̃_{t+c} = T_θ(z_t, E_a(u))`, then `D_θ(z̃)` reads
`(Δw, Δy, r, V_k, p_succ)` off the **predicted** latent.

## v121 — deployment tape

64 episodes, `chain1b_lr2`, c = 10, one triple `(z_t, u_t, z_{t+c})` per chunk
boundary. **1,548 triples**, latent dim 2048, 15,970 env steps. `z` is the pooled
PrefixVLM prefix hidden under the full prompt; the target is the latent at
`t+c`. Nothing is reconstructed anywhere.

## v122 — `T_θ`, and the confound it exposed

Episode-disjoint split, 1,158 train / 390 test. Residual parameterisation so the
identity floor is explicit.

| model | test MSE | × identity |
|---|---:|---:|
| identity (`z̃ = z_t`) | 0.67112 | 1.000 |
| **action** `T(z_t, E_a(u))` | **0.37357** | **0.557** |
| no-action `T(z_t)` | 0.37707 | 0.562 |
| shuffled action | 0.39623 | 0.590 |

Paired bootstrap over the 16 held-out **episodes** (triples within an episode are
not independent):

| quantity | value | 95% CI | verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| action gain over no-action | +0.00353 | **[−0.00172, +0.00892]** | **not significant** |
| counterfactual sensitivity | +0.02276 | [+0.01949, +0.02645] | significant |

**The model uses the action but gains nothing from it.** `T_θ` predicts the future
latent well (0.557 × identity), and shuffling the action measurably degrades it —
yet an action-free model does just as well.

The cause is a property of the data, not the model: under a fixed policy
`u ≈ π(z)`, so given `z_t` the action carries no independent information and
action-conditioned dynamics are **unidentifiable**. This is exactly the trap the
first session fell into in another form — a number that looks like success
(0.557 × identity) while the mechanism being claimed is absent.

## v123 — chain1b has outcome variance to act on

16 states × 8 rollouts, 31,265 steps.

| quantity | chain2b | **chain1b** |
|---|---:|---:|
| within-state variance | 0 (`p_cream` = 0/352) | **0.1191** |
| between-state variance | — | 0.1111 |
| **within fraction** | 0 | **0.517** |

**Over half the outcome variance is within a fixed initial state.** So a `p_succ`

## v124 — collection with action diversity

Per-chunk σ drawn from {0, 0.6, 1.5, 3.0}, 96 episodes, seeds 6300–6395,
disjoint from both earlier ranges. Varying σ *within* episodes decorrelates `u`
from `z`, which is what makes `T_θ`'s action input identifiable.

This is **not** a way around the problem: action-conditioned dynamics cannot be
learned from data where the action is a function of the state, at any model
capacity. Exploration during deployment is the cost of learning the model, and it
will lower the success rate *during collection*. That cost is recorded, not
hidden.

## v129/v131 — and an implementation defect that invalidated two nulls

At σ=3, depth 4 the candidates are genuinely different, which is the setting where
a world model should earn its keep: propose diverse (some bad) actions, filter
with the model.

| arm | success |
|---|---:|
| random σ=3 d4 | 17/48 = 0.354 |
| wm σ=3 d4 | 14/48 = 0.292 |

Then v130 found the defect: **ranking was done on `sigmoid(logit)`, which
saturated.**

| σ | depth | logit sd | #distinct after sigmoid | #distinct logits |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 1 | 0.078 | **1.0 of 8** | 8.0 |
| 3.0 | 4 | 0.760 | **2.0 of 8** | 8.0 |

The eight candidates collapsed to a single float, so `argmax` was ranking
identical values. **The v126 and v129 nulls measured a ranking destroyed by my own
code.** Re-run on logits:

| arm | success | vs matched random |
|---|---:|---|
| WM-logit σ=0 d1 | 20/48 = 0.417 | worse than random's 23/48 |
| WM-logit σ=3 d4 | 14/48 = 0.292 | **+8 −11, p = 0.648** |

The bug was real and is fixed (8/8 distinct logits, range median 0.187 / 2.88).
**The conclusion is unchanged, but it is now an honest measurement.**

Also correcting an earlier misreading of mine: a uniform argmax **index**
distribution is not evidence of a broken ranker. Candidates are exchangeable and
the index is arbitrary, so uniformity is expected either way. The evidence is the
paired comparison, not the index histogram.

## v132 — is there any leverage to select? (running)

Before tuning `T_θ` or `D_θ` further: execute each of the 8 candidates from the
same state by deterministic replay and compare outcomes. If every candidate leads
to the same place, no scorer can help and none of the nulls above say anything
about the world model.

This is the chain1b analogue of chain2b's `p_cream` probe. **v123's within-state
variance of 0.517 does not imply per-boundary leverage** — it accumulates over
~25 decision points across a whole episode. Conflating the two would be the same
error as reading v125's shuffled-action spread (0.0283) as the candidate spread
(0.0027).

## v132 — selection DOES have leverage on chain1b

Each of the 8 candidates executed from the same state by deterministic replay,
then the frozen policy to the horizon.

| σ | random-pick | **oracle-pick** | ceiling gain | states where candidates disagree |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 0.469 | **0.688** | **+0.219** | 8/16 |
| 3.0 | 0.562 | **0.875** | **+0.312** | 12/16 |

**A perfect scorer at a single boundary would move 0.469 → 0.688.** So every
deployment null above is about the *scorer*, not about having nothing to score —
the opposite of chain2b, where `p_cream` was 0/352.

This also **retracts my own earlier guess** that per-boundary selection lacks
leverage. It does have leverage; I had only shown that episode-level variance does
not imply it.

And it exposes a design error in v126/v129/v131: at σ=3 injected **once**,
random-pick is 0.562 — *better* than σ=0's 0.469. In the deployment arms I injected
σ=3 at every one of ~25 boundaries, compounding noise, and scored 0.354. Diversity
belongs at a few branch points, not everywhere.

## v134 — the LCState was missing the component actions control

Root cause of the flat ranking: `T_θ`'s action gain was 0.81% of identity, so
`T_θ(z,u) ≈ T_θ(z)` and all candidates produced the same predicted latent. The
pooled prefix hidden is a language-vision alignment representation that barely
moves in 10 steps. Proprioception — eef pose, gripper, joints, 25 dims — is what
an action actually controls, and it was absent.

Trained **separately** per component:

| component | identity | action | no-action | shuffled | action gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| pooled hidden (2048) | 0.649 | 0.552× | 0.560× | 0.597× | +0.81% |
| **proprioception (25)** | 0.682 | **0.117×** | 0.235× | **1.217×** | **+11.78%** |

CI [+5.74%, +10.91%]. **On proprioception `T_θ` is a genuine dynamics model:**
knowing the action halves the error, and feeding it the *wrong* action makes
prediction worse than not predicting at all (1.217× identity) — the signature a
smoother cannot produce.

The earlier +0.81% was the 2048-d hidden drowning the 25-d signal: the loss is a
mean over dims, so proprio carried ~1.2% of the gradient. **I had added per-
component *reporting* but not per-component *optimisation*, and nearly condemned a
world model that works.** Balancing the loss lifts proprio to +4.01%; training it
alone gives +11.78%.

## v135 — a good dynamics model still does not rank

Architecture forced by the above: head reads `[T_θ(proprio, u), current hidden]`
— prediction for the component that responds to actions, current value for the one
that does not.

| head | held-out AUC | within-state ranking AUC (12 configs) |
|---|---:|---|
| `Δw` | 0.997 | 0.409 – 0.493 |
| `p_succ` | 0.645 | 0.472 – 0.523 |

Still chance. The diagnosis is now specific: **the heads discriminate *where a
state sits in the task*, not *which of several near-identical actions is better*.**
They are trained on episode-level labels carried back to every triple, so they are
never asked to separate candidates at one state, and have no discriminative power
in that direction.

## SCOPE CORRECTION — which negative results survive, and which do not

The user pointed out that the world model was built the wrong way throughout, so
conclusions drawn from it were premature. Splitting them:

### Scoped to a broken construction — do NOT cite as evidence about latent WMs

| conclusion | why it is now scoped |
|---|---|
| "the rollout adds nothing at deployment" (wm 75/128 vs pre_enc 72/128, p = 0.728) | measured on a **frozen** latent with an MSE-to-exact-next-latent transition, trained in three separate stages |
| "the heads are flat along the direction actions move" | a *consequence* of the frozen latent, not a property of latent WMs |
| "multi-step recursion does not help" (H=4 0.609, H=8 0.600) | same broken transition, and `π̂` had only 584 on-policy triples |
| "`T_θ`'s action gain is only +0.81% on the pooled hidden" | correct about *that* representation; says nothing about a learned one |

### Independent of world-model design — these stand

| result | why it stands |
|---|---|
| chain2b `p_cream` = 0/352 (ODE + 4 SDE settings) | a measurement of the **policy's** action distribution |
| chain1b oracle ceiling +0.219 (σ=0) / +0.312 (σ=3) | a measurement of the **environment and policy** |
| within-state outcome variance 0.517 on chain1b vs 0 on chain2b | ditto |
| candidate selection helps: 0.430 → 0.586, p = 0.0078, replicated (panel C p = 0.0022) | a property of the deployed **system**, whatever produces the score |
| branching alone does not help (random vs base, p = 0.771) | ditto |

### The design error, named

The latent was the frozen pooled VLM prefix hidden — a language-vision **alignment**
representation. Nothing ever asked it to be action-predictable, and it measurably
is not (+0.81% over 10 steps). The transition then regressed the **exact** next
latent under MSE, spending capacity on decision-irrelevant variance; and the three
stages were separate, so the latent was never shaped by any value objective.

A latent world model does not need the exact next latent. It needs a **transition
distribution** over a latent that is only required to be *sufficient for value*.
That is the TD-MPC / Dreamer construction, and I built none of it — I wrote the
model from first principles instead of reading how these are actually built.

### Process correction

Search the literature **before** choosing an architecture, not only before choosing
a task. The one survey run this project (2026-08-27) asked "how is a WM used at
deployment", never "how is a latent WM built". `CLAUDE.md` gets this rule.

## Limitations not previously stated

### The labels are privileged, even though the trajectories are not

Every experiment so far describes its data as "collected during deployment". That
is true of the **trajectories** and false of the **labels**:

| signal | source | available at real deployment? |
|---|---|---|
| `Δw` (goal-atom progress) | `GoalAutomaton` + `predicate_bits` over BDDL predicates | **no** — privileged simulator state |
| branch / episode success | `env` `is_success` | only via a success detector or a human |
| `z`, `u`, proprioception | observations | yes |

v153 drops `Δw` entirely and keeps only episode success, which a deployed system
could plausibly obtain. The cost is a much sparser signal, and the survey's point
that reward and value terms are what prevent latent collapse without a decoder
means this weakens the anti-collapse mechanism at the same time.

### Branching uses a simulator affordance

The 2,048 candidate-level labels (v136, 506k steps) were produced by **deterministic
replay** — resetting to the same seed and committing to each candidate in turn. A
real robot cannot be rewound. Supervision of that kind is available in simulation
only; without it, `D_θ` must learn from single-trajectory returns, which is the
setting where ranking AUC measured ~0.5.

| 1 | frozen latent, MSE to exact next latent | deployment, `pre_enc` keeps the pretrained action encoder | 75/128 vs 72/128, **p = 0.728** |
| 2 | same | offline within-group ranking AUC | 0.637 vs 0.636 |
| 3 | jointly trained, L2 norm, 1-step | — | 0.609 / 0.583; the stochastic variant **collapsed** (identity 0.0004) |
| 4 | TD bootstrap through `T`, learned encoder | direct critic, no bootstrapping | −0.0102, direction 9/21, sign p = 0.66 |
| 5 | TD bootstrap, frozen encoder, 3-step | same | −0.0055, 95% CI [−0.0229, +0.0126] |

Spanning frozen vs learned latents, one-step vs multi-step, MSE vs TD, offline vs
deployed. Both alternative explanations were tested rather than asserted:
`--encoder frozen` removes representation learning (both arms rise to 0.629/0.635,
so learning a 256-d encoder from 4,644 transitions **was** mildly harmful — the
explanation was partly right), and `--nstep 3` shortens the bootstrap chain from
the 192 grounded terminals. Neither changes the transition comparison.

### The structural reason, which is not a data problem

At a branch point every candidate shares the same `z`. So `T(z, u)` is a
deterministic feature map of `(z, u)` and lies **inside** the function class of a
critic fed `(z, E_a(u))` directly. More data cannot make a subset outperform the
class containing it; it helps the critic at least as much. Ranking needs a critic,
and a critic does not need dynamics.

### What this does and does not license

**Does:** on `chain1b_lr2`, under the constraint that the VLA is the actor and the
model may only rank what it proposes, an action-conditioned latent transition has
no measurable contribution to ranking or to deployment success.

**Does not:** it is not evidence that latent world models are useless, nor that a
transition cannot help a VLA. The one use that would overturn it is untested here —
**using the transition to OPTIMISE action sequences** (MPPI/CEM), which is how
TD-MPC's model earns its keep. That changes what is executed: the action would no
longer be a VLA sample but a model-refined chunk. Weights and prompt stay frozen,
but the deployment semantics change, so it is not run without a decision on that.

### What does work, stated with its conditions

Selecting among 32 VLA-proposed candidates at two branch points with a learned
scorer raises success from 0.430 (matched random selection) to 0.586 on a
fit-disjoint 128-seed panel (+36 −16, p = 0.0078), replicated at 0.422 → 0.602 on a
second panel (+38 −15, p = 0.0022), and 0.453 → 0.586 against no branching
(+30 −13, p = 0.0137). Approximately 0.031 after correcting for the four
post-bugfix deployment comparisons. The scorer's features come from world-model
training (the pretrained action encoder), but the **transition** is not what
produces the gain.

## v157/v163 — policy improvement in imagination: the predicted failure, realised

The world model used for POLICY IMPROVEMENT rather than ranking. A bounded residual
`Δ_φ(z)` added to the VLA's chunk, trained purely on imagined latent rollouts, with
the VLA as prior and trust region. This is the first use in which the transition is
load-bearing by construction — a critic cannot generate a rollout.

The WM used was chosen by the v159 quality probe, not by ranking AUC:

| | mlp (learned encoder) | frozen (fixed projection) |
|---|---|---|
| downstream ranking AUC | 0.584 | **0.626** |
| h=1 prediction vs identity | **0.530×** | 0.935× |
| h=1 shuffled action | 0.617× | **0.998×** |
| value gap at h=8 | **+0.021** | +0.188 |
| corr(V_imagined, V_real) | **0.947 → 0.878** | 0.676 → 0.431 |

**Ranking AUC and predictive quality are anti-correlated here.** The model I had
been using throughout (frozen) has essentially no action-conditioned dynamics — a
shuffled action costs it 0.2% at h=1. Selecting models by ranking AUC when ranking
does not need dynamics is exactly the error named earlier, now measured.

### Deployment, fresh panel 7500–7595, disjoint from all nine fitting ranges

| arm | success | vs base |
|---|---:|---|
| base (VLA alone) | 44/96 = 0.458 | — |
| residual, imagination | **19/96 = 0.198** | +3 −28, **p < 0.0001** |

**The residual is catastrophically worse than doing nothing**, and the transition
still shows no advantage.

The failure mode was written into the actor script before the run — *"the residual
will exploit model error, scoring well in imagination and doing nothing in the
environment"* — and it materialised more strongly than that: +0.014 imagined,
−0.26 real. Both actors saturated 96% of the norm bound, pushing as hard as
permitted in a direction the value model rewards and the environment punishes.

### A blind spot in my own probe

v159 measured `corr(V_imagined, V_real) = 0.947` — but **on real trajectories**. The
residual's whole purpose is to move actions off that distribution, and the probe
never measured V anywhere the residual would actually go. A high on-policy value
correlation says nothing about value accuracy under the perturbation being
optimised. The probe answered "is the model good?" and not "is the value trustworthy
where the optimiser will push it?"

### Testbed drift, second instance

`chain1b` measures 0.39–0.60 across seed ranges — **middling, not low**. The
genuinely low-success task is `chain3` at 0.031, which I closed on the grounds that
`p_succ` could not be learned from ~2/64 positives. That reasoning no longer holds:
the current pipeline uses TD bootstrapping (sparse terminals are what bootstrapping
is for) and trains the residual in imagination, needing no candidate-level labels.
This is the same drift `CLAUDE.md` lists as failure mode #1.
