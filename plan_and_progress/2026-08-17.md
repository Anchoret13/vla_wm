## Decision: the scientific object has changed

The old mainline asked whether world-model training could transform an
imitation-sufficient VLA representation into a recurrent predictive state and
then improve one final `N=1` policy through a fixed offline vertical slice.

The new mainline asks whether a pretrained VLA can improve from its own
deployment-rollout failures by using targeted alternative-action interaction
to acquire the missing local evidence, update a decision-relevant world model,
and distill improvements back into the deployed policy over a bounded number
of rounds:

\[
\pi_0
\rightarrow \mathcal B_0
\rightarrow M_1
\rightarrow \pi_1
\rightarrow \mathcal B_1
\rightarrow M_2
\rightarrow \pi_2.
\]

Failure is an anchor-selection mechanism. It is not a reward label, an
inverse-RL signal, or proof that representation error caused the failure.
Executed sibling branches supply the intervention evidence.

The contribution is therefore the **improvement protocol and its causal
attribution**, not a particular recurrent memory, pooling layer, or policy
adapter. LCState remains the first implementation substrate, but an explicit
short visual-action window is an allowed prospectively registered fallback if
the recurrent carry remains functionally inert.

The final deployment contract is unchanged: full-prompt, recurrent or windowed
`N=1` VLA inference, with no external planner, snapshot restore, ensemble, task
automaton, or best-of-N selector at deployment.

## Why the branch changes here

The prior line established useful negative evidence and infrastructure but did
not establish model-based policy improvement:

1. V7.3 completed a data-to-policy behavior slice, but its policy state was
   dominated by a common mode, its generated-target schedule concentrated on
   one anchor, and that target had no verified realized advantage.
2. V7.4A showed that centering could expose current observation/language
   identity without making the recurrent history operational.
3. V7.4B did not acquire the positive/nonpositive sibling support needed by
   the intended model teacher, especially at late blockers.
4. V7.5 transiently opened the recurrent channel, then collapsed at the final
   checkpoint and recorded `0/72` held-out action-effect wins over copy.
5. V7.6/V7.7 showed that recovery yield was strongly controlled by acquisition
   instrument and horizon: the 30/60-action protocols truncated a capability
   whose first observed successes occurred only after 66 actions.

The combined result is not that language-conditioned latent dynamics are
false. It is that a fixed bank collected from a near-zero-success policy cannot
be assumed to contain the local interventions required for policy improvement,
and architecture refinement cannot manufacture those missing outcomes.

## What is reusable and what is new

### Reuse as implementation infrastructure

- pi0.5 runner, PrefixVLM tap, action normalization, and stochastic chunk
  sampling;
- simulator snapshot/restore, replay-fidelity checks, common-random-number
  branch execution, and video/trace capture;
- task automata, ordered progress, damage, success, and multi-horizon outcome
  decoding;
- LCState `E_a/T/U`, next-latent and physical/task heads as the initial model;
- zero-initialized policy-state injection, stock-initialized action output,
  first-`c` credit, suffix trust, demonstration rehearsal, and retention trust;
- source-disjoint ledgers, target sealing, matched-random assignments, and
  artifact hashing.

### New mainline modules

1. round-indexed rollout recorder and retrospective failure-anchor extractor;
2. arm-independent candidate-pool builder with executed-action deduplication;
3. budgeted random, diversity, uncertainty, and conservative-improvement
   selectors;
4. cumulative round dataset with policy/checkpoint/value-policy lineage;
5. policy-indexed value estimation and bootstrap uncertainty calibration;
6. policy updater that starts from `pi_k`, not stock initialization;
7. per-round behavior, interaction-AUC, calibration, and failure-frontier
   reporting.

The existing `v074_*`, `v075_*`, `v076_*`, and `v077_*` entry points should not
be edited into this orchestration. Reusable primitives should be called or
extracted into new versioned modules so the historical paths remain runnable.

## Design closures required before any new execution

These are specification blockers, not implementation details.

### C1. Executed-action causal contract

The VLA proposes a `K=50` chunk but the environment commits at most `c=10`
actions before replanning. Every branch transition, effect label, and
branch-derived policy target must therefore consume
`u_exec = u[:, 0:c]` together with an `executed_mask`. The unexecuted suffix may
receive stock trust but may not enter the predicted next-state transition or
receive branch credit. Early termination inside `c` must shorten the mask.

### C2. Bootstrap and round indexing

The current design writes `B_0 -> M_1`, while also naming a model-guided
selector in round 0 before a calibrated model exists. It also defines `D_k`
with only branches `j < k`, which excludes the newly collected `B_k` from the
model that is supposed to follow it.

Before collection, choose and write one unambiguous convention. The preferred
candidate is:

1. collect a common coverage-only bootstrap tranche `B_boot` under `pi_0`;
2. fit and calibrate `M_0` without updating the policy;
3. begin causal acquisition round 0 from a common `pi_0`, anchor panel, and
   candidate pool using `M_0` versus matched random/diversity selection;
4. define
   `D_{k+1} = D_k union D_nominal,k union B_k`, then train `M_{k+1}` and
   update `pi_{k+1}`.

No `WM-acquisition` arm may exist before the model used by its selector has
been trained and calibrated on source-disjoint data.

### C3. Continuation and policy-indexed value

Every branch must record the proposal policy, the first-`c` execution policy,
the closed-loop continuation policy, and all checkpoint hashes. If the target
is `V_k = V^{pi_k}`, returns generated under an older `pi_j` cannot be pooled as
current-policy value labels without explicit revaluation.

Action 0 must lock one initial estimator:

- current-policy Monte Carlo on sufficiently long continuations;
- current-policy TD/FQE using stored continuation transitions; or
- a separately indexed value head per generating policy.

Historical branches may remain cumulative dynamics/reward data even when their
old continuation return is masked out of the current value loss.

### C4. Operational failure-anchor rule

"The earliest decision after which the policy cannot recover" is not directly
observed from terminal failure. The extractor must specify:

- which decision snapshots are retained during the source rollout;
- how the last valid progress and stalled window are computed;
- whether retrospective continuation replay is required;
- the number of repeats and the recovery criterion;
- how anchor-search interaction is charged to the budget.

A convenient last-progress heuristic may be used, but it must not be called an
earliest-unrecoverable guarantee unless the retrospective test was executed.

### C5. One preference contract

Verified corrections currently use lexicographic task outcomes, while model
targets use scalar `r + gamma^c V`. The design must either derive the scalar
reward so that it preserves the registered safety/success/progress ordering or
apply the same component-wise conservative dominance rule to both channels.
The success and safety heads may not be trained and then ignored by selection.

The uncertainty penalty must be defined for the relative advantage
`Q(u_i) - Q(u_0)`, including reference uncertainty and covariance, rather than
as an unnamed candidate-only scale.

### C6. Causal arms and data ownership

Round 0 can provide exact matched selector comparisons only when the policies,
anchors, candidate pools, branch budgets, continuation budgets, and seeds are
identical before selection. After policy arms diverge, later-round results are
end-to-end algorithm comparisons, not pure same-data estimates of one
component.

The round-aware source table must state whether a matched-random branch is:

- executed only in a separate random arm;
- executed as shadow assessment but hidden from the method arm; or
- shared as model-training data.

These choices are not equivalent. No outcome or branch may cross arms or roles
unless that sharing is explicitly part of every compared method.

The required controls must also include:

- a zero-state/matched-boundary control when state conditioning is claimed;
- a diversity-only selector for the initial acquisition comparison;
- a delayed one-shot policy update that receives matched total failure data and
  interaction budget if iterative improvement is claimed to beat one-shot
  adaptation.

### C7. Interaction accounting and source sealing

The interaction denominator must count, in environment steps:

- acquisition rollouts used to find failures;
- any retrospective anchor-search continuations;
- repeated reference branches;
- every selected alternative branch;
- all branch continuations;
- any assessment execution used to certify selectors or model targets.

Snapshot restoration itself is not an environment step, but steps executed
after restoration are. Compute used to sample or score an unexecuted candidate
is reported separately.

Training, calibration, selector assessment, verified correction,
model-generated target, and behavior-evaluation sources must be separated by
source episode and replay ancestry within every round. Sealed target outcomes
may enter a later round only under a prospective, explicit role transition.

## New action items

### Action 0 - close and version the executable design (immediate)

No GPU execution begins before this item is complete.

Deliverables:

1. assign the new branch a non-colliding version and record the committed old
   `v0.9` as the pre-pivot one-shot LC-Flow design;
2. repair the mojibake in `framework_design.md` and add the omitted V7.5-V7.7
   evidence without changing historical outcomes;
3. resolve C1-C7 above in equations, round pseudocode, source-role tables, and
   artifact schemas;
4. make the bootstrap phase and the first legal WM-vs-random comparison
   explicit;
5. define the full required arm matrix, including zero-state, diversity-only,
   and matched-total-data one-shot controls where their corresponding claims
   are retained;
6. write the exact promotion/halt contract for benchmark calibration and the
   bootstrap collection before inspecting new outcomes.

PASS requires every symbol in the improvement sequence to map to a producing
stage, an allowed input dataset, a policy/model checkpoint, and an interaction
ledger. No selector may use a model that does not yet exist; no value label may
silently change policy identity; no comparison may share outcome data
asymmetrically.

Failure to close any of these items halts at design. It does not authorize an
implementation guess.

### Action 1 - select and freeze the mechanism-development benchmark

Run stock full-prompt `N=1` pi0.5 only. Screen existing Chain3/4/5 and
controlled shorter variants on calibration-only seeds, then confirm finalists
on a larger disjoint calibration panel fixed before confirmation.

The current small record is only a warning, not the new calibration: stock
full-prompt success is `0/5` on each existing Chain3, Chain4, and Chain5 panel;
only decomposed-prompt Chain3 reached `1/5`. The new benchmark therefore may
not assume that the existing chains already meet the target competence range.

Select tasks with measured nonzero competence and material headroom, initially
targeting the framework's estimated `20-70%` success range. Freeze task BDDL,
instructions, episode horizon, acquisition seeds, calibration seeds,
behavior-evaluation seeds, and all budget units. Calibration episodes never
become training anchors or behavior-evaluation episodes.

HALT if no candidate task set provides both nonzero stock success and multiple
failure opportunities. In that case change the task difficulty before building
the world model; do not compensate with privileged late-state reachers inside
the primary mechanism benchmark.

### Action 2 - collect the common bootstrap acquisition object

From stock-policy failures on acquisition-only seeds:

1. save the complete reproducible history, simulator/controller snapshot,
   task-monitor state, policy checkpoint/hash, action history, and failure
   localization trace;
2. build one outcome-blind candidate pool per anchor from supported pi0.5
   stochastic chunks and prospectively bounded perturbations;
3. deduplicate by first-`c` executed action and replay-noise tolerance;
4. repeat `u_0`, then execute fixed diversity and matched-random candidates
   under common-random-number control;
5. retain every attempted branch and charge all executed steps;
6. report better-than-reference, tied, worse, damage, effect-diversity, and
   continuation-outcome support by task and failure phase.

This tranche is coverage bootstrap, not a WM-acquisition result. It does not
train the policy.

PASS requires reproducible reference replay plus both improving and
non-improving siblings on at least two selected tasks, with source-disjoint
groups reserved for model calibration and assessment. Exact quotas and branch
budgets are frozen by Action 0 before collection.

HALT if the proposal pool has no realized outcome variation or no supported
positive correction. Change the benchmark or proposal mechanism before any WM
or policy training.

### Action 3 - train the minimal counterfactual model

Reuse the current latent model as the first substrate. Do not begin with a
pooling, layer, rank, or broad architecture sweep.

Train next-latent, physical effect, task progress/reward, current-policy value,
sibling ranking, and the language/physics contract. Add the prospectively
chosen bootstrap ensemble and calibrate relative-advantage uncertainty and
local support on source-disjoint executed sibling groups.

Promotion requires beating copy-state, no-action, stock-action, and empirical
frequency baselines on held-out sibling groups, together with usable ranking
calibration. Aggregate training loss or recurrence liveness alone cannot pass.

### Action 4 - test acquisition before policy learning

Using the same frozen policy checkpoint, new source-disjoint anchors, common
candidate pools, and equal executed-step budgets, seal model-guided,
diversity-only, and random selections before revealing outcomes.

Primary readouts are verified improvements per executed branch, realized
advantage magnitude, top-1 regret, damage, coverage, and calibration. This is
the clean test of whether the WM improves interaction efficiency.

If the model does not beat matched random/diversity, retain any supported
counterfactual-prediction result but do not promote a model-guided acquisition
claim or proceed nominally to the full method.

### Action 5 - run the first policy update

From matched initialization and role-disjoint ledgers, compare at minimum:

- stock pi0.5;
- one-shot nominal/demo fine-tuning;
- failure-random plus verified corrections;
- WM acquisition plus verified corrections;
- failure-random plus verified and model-generated targets;
- full WM acquisition plus verified and model-generated targets;
- the registered zero-state/matched-boundary control.

Only the executed first-`c` actions receive branch/model credit. Model targets
are sealed outcome-blind inside calibrated support; exact ties and unsupported
anchors receive zero model loss. Evaluate every resulting policy on the frozen
behavior panel with full-prompt `N=1` inference.

### Action 6 - run round 2 and the matched one-shot control

Collect new failures separately from each `pi_1` arm, preserve arm-specific
lineage, update its WM/value estimate, and update to `pi_2` under the same
task-level interaction budget. Report the failure frontier after each round.

Compare the iterative update with a prospectively defined delayed one-shot
control receiving matched total interaction and failure data. Later-round arm
differences are reported as end-to-end improvement trajectories; only the
common-policy selector experiment from Action 4 supports a clean acquisition
attribution.

### Action 7 - promote only after a replicated mechanism win

Promotion to the five-task public LoHo stress test requires:

1. failure-anchored branches add held-out action-effect/outcome support;
2. WM-guided acquisition beats matched random/diversity per interaction;
3. the final `N=1` policy beats stock and correction-only controls;
4. model-generated targets add value beyond the same verified corrections, if
   that stronger claim is retained;
5. round 2 maintains or improves behavior and moves the failure frontier;
6. standard-task retention and irreversible damage remain within the frozen
   tolerance.

If only verified-correction arms improve, the supported result is
failure-driven corrective imitation. If the model improves acquisition but
not unexecuted targets, retain the active-acquisition claim and drop the
model-generated-teacher claim. Full LoHo is not opened merely because an
offline model metric improved.
