## Summary

Action 2M.2 asks for effect-stratified selection over raw stock-PI0 draws. Two
measurements say that intervention cannot be delivered, and a third says the
result it was meant to repair was never there to begin with.

1. the raw 64-draw proposal pool is **effect-degenerate** at the anchors
   `tau=160` admits, so effect strata cannot be populated;
2. one of the three registered stratification axes — gripper-close timing — is
   **unfillable at 46/48 anchors**;
3. **the sibling ranking signal in `B_boot-v2` is at or below its own
   continuation-seed noise floor.** This retracts a claim I made on 2026-08-21.

Four independent refutation attempts, one of them arguing explicitly for
running 2M.2 as written, returned **0 refutations**: two recommended halting as

## 1. The raw pool is effect-degenerate
`results/v084_probe/2026-08-23T185117Z` — 8 sources, 5 eligible anchors, 64 raw
draws each, no selection applied. 1,976 env steps.

| anchor | unique first-10 | tmag CV | tmag range | gripper classes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| a3800 | 64/64 | 0.0067 | 0.251 | 1 |
| a3801 | 64/64 | 0.0121 | 0.297 | 1 |
| a3802 | 64/64 | 0.0068 | 0.255 | 1 |
| a3803 | 64/64 | 0.0214 | 0.465 | 1 |
| a3805 | 64/64 | 0.0121 | 0.316 | 1 |

Every chunk is byte-distinct and every chunk is behaviourally the same. Median
within-anchor pairwise direction angle is **3.05°** against a between-anchor
median of **31.26°**; the gripper command is saturated (`|a| > 0.95`) in
**100%** of 8,160 stored entries, and **0/5** probe anchors contain both
gripper classes.

Framework §4.1 already legislates this case: *"byte-different chunks whose
first `c` actions are replay-noise-indistinguishable count as one candidate
family."* By that registered criterion the entire 64-draw pool is **one
candidate family**, and selection over one family cannot produce strata.

Corroboration in the collected bank: `post_prefix_delta` is **bitwise
identical across all 17 candidates at 43 of 48 anchors** (within-repeat
variance 8.1e-24 against between-candidate 4.5e-9). At 90% of anchors the
executed prefix produces literally the same physical effect whichever sibling
runs.

## 2. The registered ranking signal was continuation-seed noise

This is the important one, and it corrects
[`2026-08-21.md`](2026-08-21.md) and framework §16.

On 2026-08-21 I reported that `B_boot-v2` was "the first V8 bank with two-sided
ranking signal" because its test split held 29 `better` and 19 `worse` of 384
pairs. The three test repeats make that decomposable, and it does not survive:

| contrast | non-tie rate |
|---|---:|
| candidate vs reference, **matched** continuation seed | **48/384 = 12.50%** |
| **same chunk**, different continuation seed | **78/408 = 19.12%** |
| reference against **itself** across repeats | **4/24 = 16.67%** |

**The candidate-identity effect is below its own noise floor.** Re-running an
identical action chunk under a different continuation seed flips the label more
often than swapping to a completely different chunk under a matched seed.

Per-candidate consistency confirms it: 38 of 128 test candidates are non-tied
against the reference at least once, and **0 of 128 hold the same non-tie sign
across all three repeats.** Candidate ICC on the continuous components is
`dp -0.011`, `G 0.002`, `ttm 0.227` — only `ttm` is distinguishable from zero.

A repeat-count confound explains why this was invisible: `R=1` pairs (the 40
train/val anchors) are 2.98% non-tie while `R=3` pairs (8 test anchors) are
16.27%. The test split looked richer because it was repeated, not because its
candidates differed.

`[RETRACTED]` "`B_boot-v2` produced two-sided held-out ranking support." The
pairs exist; they are not evidence of candidate-induced differences. What
`B_boot-v2` established is narrower and still useful: **at these anchors the
outcome label is dominated by continuation noise**, so no selector trained on
it could have ranked siblings, and M0.1's `3/48` non-tie accuracy is the
expected result rather than a model failure.

## 3. Scope of the claim, stated precisely

One judge corrected my framing and the correction is adopted. The supported
statement is **not** "stock PI0's iid proposals are effect-degenerate" — that
is a claim about the policy in general. It is:

> At the anchors a fixed `tau=160` on `chain1b_lr2@250` admits, proposal spread
> is small, and where it is larger it does not move the registered outcome or
> ranking readout beyond continuation-seed noise.

That bounds this collection, this task, this `tau`, and this `H`. It does not
bound PI0 or the method.

## 4. Recommendation, and what is NOT being proposed

**HALT Action 2M.2 before its ~120,000-step collection.** Effect-stratified
selection over this pool is the same intervention in new clothes, and the
daily's own out-of-scope list already excludes "another increase in iid PI0
sibling count with the same FPS/random family" for what is now a measured
reason.

Two candidate successors, both cheaper than 2M.2. At the time of the result the
choice was not taken:

- **(a) Settle the noise floor properly.** The matched-seed result rests on 8
  anchors x 3 repeats. Raising repeats to 8-16 at ~15 anchors costs far less
  than 120k steps and would establish whether *any* candidate effect exists
  above noise at this setting. If it does not, no proposal family fixes it and
  the binding constraint is the outcome readout or the anchor, not the
  candidates.
- **(b) Change the proposal family for real.** Framework §2 `[LOCKED]` already
  permits "a bounded perturbation of their supported chunks". Matching the
  pooled effect spread needs per-step deltas of roughly 20-37% of the observed
  action limit on translation, or a gripper sign flip — bounded, inside
  support, and not unconstrained action search.

(a) should precede (b): if outcomes at this anchor are noise-dominated, a more
diverse proposal family will produce more diverse actions and the same
unrankable labels.

`[DECISION 2026-08-23]` Successor (a) is now selected as **Action 2M.3** below.
This decision writes the next Action Item; it does not authorize execution.

Explicitly **not** proposed: another benchmark screen, horizon audit, anchor
diagnostic, architecture sweep, or a rerun of Action 2P.

## ACTION ITEM — Action 2M.3: separate candidate effect from continuation noise

**Status:** ~~`PROPOSED / NOT STARTED — awaiting explicit approval`~~ →
**EXECUTED 2026-08-23.** First attempt halted on anchor underfill (13 of 15 from
a 30-source ceiling). Re-registered at 38 sources — the only change — and
completed: 15/15 anchors, 900 branches, **`NOT_IDENTIFIED`**. Record at the foot
of this file.

**Objective:** determine whether candidate identity moves the registered H80
outcome above continuation-seed variability at the current
`chain1b_lr2@250, tau=160` setting. This is the minimum identification step
required before another proposal-family collection or M0 training run.

### Locked experiment

- **Independent unit:** 15 fresh eligible source anchors.
- **Pool:** 64 raw stock-PI0 chunks per anchor, generated outcome-blind.
- **Executed candidates:** one reference plus four chunks selected before any
  outcome to maximize action-only spread within the stock pool.
- **Repeats:** exactly 12 shared continuation seeds per anchor; every candidate
  and reference uses the same 12 keys.
- **Branch:** execute the first `c=10` actions, then stock-PI0 continuation to
  `H=80`; no change to task, anchor, outcome vector, quantization floors, or
  deadline.
- **Ownership:** permanent `calibration/noise_floor`; no row may enter WM or
  policy training, calibration of a later model, selector assessment, or
  behavior evaluation.
- **Hard ceiling:** at most 30 source episodes and
  `15 × 5 × 12 = 900` branches, for at most **88,500 environment steps**
  (`30×250 + 900×90`). No quota or cap extension.

The executable manifest must freeze source/continuation seeds, the four-
candidate selection rule, effect statistics, bootstrap keys, ledger schema,
and the 88,500-step cap before the first environment step.

### Primary comparison

For every registered outcome component, report both:

1. **candidate effect:** candidate versus reference under the same continuation
   seed;
2. **noise floor:** the identical chunk under different continuation seeds.

The primary continuous components are fixed as `dp`, `G`, and `ttm`; `D` and
ICC are computed separately for each rather than pooled or selected after the
run. Damage and success remain mandatory secondary safety/task readouts.

The primary statistic is the anchor-clustered difference

```text
D = matched-seed candidate/reference non-tie rate
    - same-chunk cross-seed non-tie rate.
```

Also report candidate ICC and per-candidate sign consistency across all 12
repeats. Repeats and candidate pairs remain nested; they are never counted as
independent anchors.

### Decision and handoff

**CANDIDATE_EFFECT_IDENTIFIED** only if, for the same primary continuous
component, the prospectively frozen one-sided 95% anchor-cluster confidence
bound for `D` is above zero and the candidate-ICC lower bound is positive. All
components, intervals, and sign-consistency counts are reported regardless of
result.

- If identified, register the bounded-perturbation proposal family next, using
  the measured effect/noise scale, and only then collect a fresh bank and train
  M0.2.
- If not identified, do not change the proposal family or train M0.2 at this
  setting. The next decision is whether to change the outcome readout or the
  anchor/setting; it is a formulation decision, not another model or proposal
  sweep.

### Required deliverables

- immutable registration and source/branch ledger;
- raw repeated outcome rows with candidate/reference/seed identities;
- `summary.json` containing `D`, confidence bounds, ICC, sign consistency, and
  exact interaction spend;
- an execution-day result entry and updated action status.

No world-model checkpoint, acquisition selector, or policy update is a
deliverable of Action 2M.3.

### The shape of the answer is not a flat null, and that matters

Three findings pull in different directions and are all real:

1. **Candidate identity does explain outcome variance.** ICC lower bounds are
   positive on every primary component, and on `G` the point estimate is
   **0.4618** with a lower bound of **0.2560** — roughly a quarter to a half of
   continuation-return variance is candidate-attributable.
2. **It does not clear the discrimination floor.** `D` — the non-tie-rate excess
   over seed noise — is positive on all three primaries and indistinguishable
   from zero on all three.
3. **No candidate holds its direction.** Sign consistency is **0/60 on every
   component**, across twelve shared seeds. 17 of 60 candidates beat or lose to
   the reference at least once on `G`; none does so consistently.

The mechanism is visible in the magnitudes. On `G`, the spread of per-candidate
*means* — the signal — has median **0.0048** across anchors, while the
within-candidate range across the 12 seeds — the noise — has median **0.0463**,
about **10x** larger. Only 3/15 anchors have a candidate-mean spread above the
registered floor of 0.0956; 5/15 have a seed range above it.

And the readout itself is coarse: **`G` takes 10 distinct values across all 900
branches**, and only **3 of 15 anchors** register any `success@250` among their
60 branches. The candidate effect is real in variance terms and smaller than the
quantum the outcome vector can express.

### Registered handoff

Per the decision rule: **do not change the proposal family and do not train
M0.2 at this setting.** The next decision is whether to change the **outcome
readout** or the **anchor/setting** — a formulation decision, not another model
or proposal sweep.

The evidence points at the readout rather than the anchor. The candidate effect
exists (ICC) but is sub-floor, and the floors are not arbitrary: they were
derived from the automaton's 10-step evaluation stride. A readout that resolves
milestone timing more finely than 10 steps, or a continuous progress measure
that does not quantize to 10 values, would let an effect of this size register.
That is a formulation change and is **not** taken here.
