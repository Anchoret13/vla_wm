Every chunk is byte-distinct and every chunk is behaviourally the same. Median

Framework §4.1 already legislates this case: *"byte-different chunks whose

Corroboration in the collected bank: `post_prefix_delta` is **bitwise
identical across all 17 candidates at 43 of 48 anchors** (within-repeat
variance 8.1e-24 against between-candidate 4.5e-9). At 90% of anchors the
executed prefix produces literally the same physical effect whichever sibling
runs.

## 2. The registered ranking signal was continuation-seed noise

This is the important one, and it corrects
[`2026-08-21.md`](2026-08-21.md) and framework §16.

On 2026-08-21 I reported that `B_boot-v2` was "the first V8 bank with two-sided
ranking signal" because its test split held 29 `better` and 19 `worse` of 384
pairs. The three test repeats make that decomposable, and it does not survive:

| contrast | non-tie rate |
|---|---:|
| candidate vs reference, **matched** continuation seed | **48/384 = 12.50%** |
| **same chunk**, different continuation seed | **78/408 = 19.12%** |
| reference against **itself** across repeats | **4/24 = 16.67%** |

**The candidate-identity effect is below its own noise floor.** Re-running an
identical action chunk under a different continuation seed flips the label more
often than swapping to a completely different chunk under a matched seed.

Per-candidate consistency confirms it: 38 of 128 test candidates are non-tied
against the reference at least once, and **0 of 128 hold the same non-tie sign
across all three repeats.** Candidate ICC on the continuous components is
`dp -0.011`, `G 0.002`, `ttm 0.227` — only `ttm` is distinguishable from zero.

A repeat-count confound explains why this was invisible: `R=1` pairs (the 40
train/val anchors) are 2.98% non-tie while `R=3` pairs (8 test anchors) are
16.27%. The test split looked richer because it was repeated, not because its
candidates differed.

`[RETRACTED]` "`B_boot-v2` produced two-sided held-out ranking support." The
pairs exist; they are not evidence of candidate-induced differences. What
`B_boot-v2` established is narrower and still useful: **at these anchors the
outcome label is dominated by continuation noise**, so no selector trained on
it could have ranked siblings, and M0.1's `3/48` non-tie accuracy is the
expected result rather than a model failure.

## 3. Scope of the claim, stated precisely

One judge corrected my framing and the correction is adopted. The supported
statement is **not** "stock PI0's iid proposals are effect-degenerate" — that
is a claim about the policy in general. It is:

> At the anchors a fixed `tau=160` on `chain1b_lr2@250` admits, proposal spread
> is small, and where it is larger it does not move the registered outcome or
> ranking readout beyond continuation-seed noise.

That bounds this collection, this task, this `tau`, and this `H`. It does not
bound PI0 or the method.

## 4. Recommendation, and what is NOT being proposed

**HALT Action 2M.2 before its ~120,000-step collection.** Effect-stratified
selection over this pool is the same intervention in new clothes, and the
daily's own out-of-scope list already excludes "another increase in iid PI0
sibling count with the same FPS/random family" for what is now a measured
reason.

Two candidate successors, both cheaper than 2M.2. At the time of the result the
choice was not taken:

- **(a) Settle the noise floor properly.** The matched-seed result rests on 8
  anchors x 3 repeats. Raising repeats to 8-16 at ~15 anchors costs far less
  than 120k steps and would establish whether *any* candidate effect exists
  above noise at this setting. If it does not, no proposal family fixes it and
  the binding constraint is the outcome readout or the anchor, not the
  candidates.
- **(b) Change the proposal family for real.** Framework §2 `[LOCKED]` already
  permits "a bounded perturbation of their supported chunks". Matching the
  pooled effect spread needs per-step deltas of roughly 20-37% of the observed
  action limit on translation, or a gripper sign flip — bounded, inside
  support, and not unconstrained action search.

(a) should precede (b): if outcomes at this anchor are noise-dominated, a more
diverse proposal family will produce more diverse actions and the same
unrankable labels.

  `selector_assess` by source split. All v085 rows remain
  `calibration/noise_floor` and enter none of these splits.

### Training readout

Reuse the registered phase-aware potential in `lcwm/phase_potential.py` and
evaluate it at every environment step rather than at the old ten-step automaton
cadence. Store the raw trace and derive, per candidate and repeat:

- post-prefix physical delta;
- `ΔΦ_exec = Φ(tau+c) - Φ(tau)`;
- continuation `ΔΦ` at `H=20, 40, 80`;
- discounted continuous progress return over the continuation;
- damage and success, with the old `dp/ttm/G` vector retained as secondary
  reporting rather than the primary training/ranking label.

`Φ` is initialized from the source episode, not rebased at the branch anchor.
The collector records the source's per-step `q`, object positions, predicate
bits, object names, and goal atoms through `tau`, builds the phase-potential
memos on that history, and forks those memos with the environment snapshot for
every candidate/repeat. Reinitializing approach, attachment, lift, or transport
state at `tau` is prohibited.

Let `Φ_j = Φ(tau+j)`, `gamma=0.99`, and hold `Φ` constant after an early
terminal transition. The canonical continuous action-consequence score is

```text
GΦ_exec(i,r)    = sum[j=1..c] gamma^(j-1) * (Φ_j - Φ_(j-1))
GΦ_cont(i,r,H)  = sum[j=1..H] gamma^(j-1) * (Φ_(c+j) - Φ_(c+j-1))
QΦ(i,r)         = GΦ_exec(i,r) + gamma^c * GΦ_cont(i,r,80)
S(i)            = mean_r QΦ(i,r)
delta(i)        = S(i) - S(reference)
```

`GΦ_cont` at 20 and 40 is auxiliary; `QΦ` at 80 is the unique target for the
paired-effect loss and top-1 readout. Held-out top-1 regret is
`max_i S(i) - S(argmax_i predicted_S(i))`. Damage and success are reported for
the selected candidate and retain their safety/task meaning, but no policy
target is admitted by this M0.2 training action.

The bank retains every repeat row. The deterministic M0.2 target for one
`(state, candidate)` is the **candidate mean across its shared-seed repeats**.
The action-effect objective regresses the candidate-minus-reference mean
consequence directly. Do not turn the repeats into conflicting per-rollout
tie/better/worse labels for one deterministic model output.

### Train once, then open held-out test once

1. Reuse the M0.1 state/action architecture and fixed 30-epoch optimizer
   schedule; do not run an architecture or hyperparameter sweep.
2. Train the action-conditioned model and an identically initialized,
   capacity-matched state-only model. Use immediate physical-effect regression,
   continuous progress/return regression, and candidate-reference paired-effect
   regression.
3. Fit all target normalization on train sources only: RMS scaling for physical
   deltas and mean/std scaling for continuous progress targets, each clamped at
   `1e-4`. Freeze those statistics before validation.
4. Use the single fixed objective

   ```text
   L_total = 1.0 * L_physical
           + 1.0 * L_task
           + 0.25 * L_paired_effect
   ```

   where `L_physical` and continuous `L_task` terms are Smooth-L1 on normalized
   candidate-mean targets, damage/success use BCE on repeat-mean rates, and
   `L_paired_effect` is Smooth-L1 between predicted and observed normalized
   `delta(i)` for every non-reference candidate. All terms are averaged first
   within an anchor and then across anchors.
5. Select each checkpoint by minimum source-disjoint validation `L_total` with
   exactly the same formula and frozen train statistics.
6. After selection is frozen, open test once and compare against state-only,
   no-change/copy, train empirical-mean, and stock-reference predictors.
7. Report held-out physical MSE, continuous-effect MAE/RMSE and correlation,
   candidate-reference effect error, top-1 regret, action-shuffle loss gap, and
   damage/success calibration. Anchors are the independent unit; repeats are
   nested estimates of one candidate consequence.

Mechanics smoke tests may verify schema, finite losses, strict checkpoint reload,
restore, and ledger accounting. They are implementation checks only; after they
pass, the same Action proceeds to full collection and training without a new
