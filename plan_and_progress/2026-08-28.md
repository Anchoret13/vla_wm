## v122 — `T_θ`, and the confound it exposed

Episode-disjoint split, 1,158 train / 390 test. Residual parameterisation so the
identity floor is explicit.

| model | test MSE | × identity |
|---|---:|---:|
| identity (`z̃ = z_t`) | 0.67112 | 1.000 |
| **action** `T(z_t, E_a(u))` | **0.37357** | **0.557** |
| no-action `T(z_t)` | 0.37707 | 0.562 |
| shuffled action | 0.39623 | 0.590 |

Paired bootstrap over the 16 held-out **episodes** (triples within an episode are
not independent):

| quantity | value | 95% CI | verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| action gain over no-action | +0.00353 | **[−0.00172, +0.00892]** | **not significant** |
| counterfactual sensitivity | +0.02276 | [+0.01949, +0.02645] | significant |

**The model uses the action but gains nothing from it.** `T_θ` predicts the future
latent well (0.557 × identity), and shuffling the action measurably degrades it —
yet an action-free model does just as well.

The cause is a property of the data, not the model: under a fixed policy
`u ≈ π(z)`, so given `z_t` the action carries no independent information and
action-conditioned dynamics are **unidentifiable**. This is exactly the trap the
first session fell into in another form — a number that looks like success
(0.557 × identity) while the mechanism being claimed is absent.

## v124 — collection with action diversity

Per-chunk σ drawn from {0, 0.6, 1.5, 3.0}, 96 episodes, seeds 6300–6395,
disjoint from both earlier ranges. Varying σ *within* episodes decorrelates `u`
from `z`, which is what makes `T_θ`'s action input identifiable.

This is **not** a way around the problem: action-conditioned dynamics cannot be
learned from data where the action is a function of the state, at any model
capacity. Exploration during deployment is the cost of learning the model, and it
will lower the success rate *during collection*. That cost is recorded, not
hidden.

## v125 — `D_θ`'s `p_succ`, read off the predicted latent

| head input | held-out AUC |
|---|---:|
| current latent `z_t` | 0.645 |
| **predicted latent** `T_θ(z_t, u)` | 0.649 |

Rolling forward adds essentially no outcome information at `c = 10`. But the
distinction that matters is not accuracy: `p_succ(z_t)` is **constant in `u`** and
cannot rank candidates at all, while `p_succ(T_θ(z_t,u))` varies with the action.
Only the second is usable for selection — that is the line between a value
function and a world model here.

## v127 — where the signal dies

At a fixed state, sweeping candidate diversity and rollout depth (0 env steps
beyond reaching the state):

| σ | depth | action spread | latent spread | score sd |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 1 | 0.41 | 0.24 | 0.0009 |
| 0.0 | 8 | 0.41 | 0.82 | 0.0030 |
| 3.0 | 1 | 3.15 | 1.36 | 0.0050 |
| 3.0 | 8 | 3.15 | 5.43 | 0.0138 |

`T_θ` **propagates action differences well** (ratio ≈ 0.4–0.6 into latent space).
The head is what is flat: a **22×** increase in latent spread moved the score sd
by 0.0129. Cause is a horizon mismatch — `p_succ`'s label is ~200 steps distal
while one selection moves the next 10.

## v129/v131 — and an implementation defect that invalidated two nulls

At σ=3, depth 4 the candidates are genuinely different, which is the setting where
a world model should earn its keep: propose diverse (some bad) actions, filter
with the model.

| arm | success |
|---|---:|
| random σ=3 d4 | 17/48 = 0.354 |
| wm σ=3 d4 | 14/48 = 0.292 |

Then v130 found the defect: **ranking was done on `sigmoid(logit)`, which
saturated.**

| σ | depth | logit sd | #distinct after sigmoid | #distinct logits |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 1 | 0.078 | **1.0 of 8** | 8.0 |
| 3.0 | 4 | 0.760 | **2.0 of 8** | 8.0 |

The eight candidates collapsed to a single float, so `argmax` was ranking
identical values. **The v126 and v129 nulls measured a ranking destroyed by my own
code.** Re-run on logits:

| arm | success | vs matched random |
|---|---:|---|
| WM-logit σ=0 d1 | 20/48 = 0.417 | worse than random's 23/48 |
| WM-logit σ=3 d4 | 14/48 = 0.292 | **+8 −11, p = 0.648** |

The bug was real and is fixed (8/8 distinct logits, range median 0.187 / 2.88).
**The conclusion is unchanged, but it is now an honest measurement.**

Also correcting an earlier misreading of mine: a uniform argmax **index**
distribution is not evidence of a broken ranker. Candidates are exchangeable and
the index is arbitrary, so uniformity is expected either way. The evidence is the
paired comparison, not the index histogram.

## v132 — is there any leverage to select? (running)

Before tuning `T_θ` or `D_θ` further: execute each of the 8 candidates from the
same state by deterministic replay and compare outcomes. If every candidate leads
to the same place, no scorer can help and none of the nulls above say anything
about the world model.

This is the chain1b analogue of chain2b's `p_cream` probe. **v123's within-state
variance of 0.517 does not imply per-boundary leverage** — it accumulates over
~25 decision points across a whole episode. Conflating the two would be the same
error as reading v125's shuffled-action spread (0.0283) as the candidate spread
(0.0027).
