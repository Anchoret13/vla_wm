## 3. Discussion findings

### 3.1 `chain2b` is an unordered partial-order task, not an ordered chain

All 20 successful confirmation episodes completed:

```text
cream-cheese pick -> cream-cheese place ->
tomato-sauce pick -> tomato-sauce place
```

This is the reverse of the BDDL/registry listing. Canonical-order successes were
0/20. Thus `chain2b` is useful as an unordered multi-goal failure benchmark but
cannot support a claim about executing the listed sequence.

The scalar phase also aliases different physical/task states. Among the seven
`phase = 2` failures:

- five had tomato sauce pick/place achieved and both cream milestones
  unresolved;
- two had cream pick/place achieved and both tomato milestones unresolved.

The same count therefore points to mutually exclusive remaining problems.
Future aggregation, anchor selection, value conditioning, and frontier reports
must use achieved/current-valid/actionable sets. Scalar phase is display-only.
Frontier movement means contraction of the unresolved set or success, not an
increase in ordered-prefix index.

### 3.2 `chain1b` is strongly deadline-sensitive

The 12 success steps were:

```text
218, 228, 237, 238, 239, 239, 246, 247, 247, 247, 249, 250
```

Six of twelve successes occurred in the last ten steps; one occurred exactly at
250. All 18 failures also reached the 250-step cap, so 19/30 episodes terminated
exactly at the deadline. At step 240 only 6/30 episodes had succeeded.

Of the 18 failures:

- 16 emitted the tomato-pick event but did not place by the deadline;
- two did not emit the pick event.

For the 16 post-pick failures, the remaining time at the pick event was
20–60 steps. Twelve of sixteen had less remaining time than the observed
successful placement-interval q90 of approximately 47. If a ten-step candidate
prefix is executed from that boundary, the official continuation budget is
only 10–50 steps, while the frozen place-class bound is 71 and global
`H_max` is 345.

The evidence therefore supports the literal description “pick event observed,
place not completed before deadline.” It does **not** identify a stable grasp,
prove a post-pick control/dynamics defect, or prove that the useful correction
decision lies at the last-progress state. Whether an earlier deadline-aware
anchor is usable is a new empirical question.

### 3.3 The current budget derivation is complete-case and context-aliased

The V8.0 code estimated milestone q90 only from trajectories in which that
milestone was achieved. Right-censored failures did not enter the quantile.
For `chain1b`, 28/30 episodes emitted the pick event but only 12/30 completed
placement; the other 16 placement times are censored at the episode cap.

`W = 345` is also larger than the entire 250-step `chain1b` episode. A full
345-step no-progress suffix therefore cannot be observed on that task, so the
corresponding state cannot literally be called a 345-step `stall_onset`.

The `chain2b` mismatch is state-specific:

- the target modal failure state, tomato completed / cream unresolved, occurred
  five times;
- 0/5 reached cream pick in their remaining 100–250 steps;
- the observed cream-pick q90 underlying the frozen 209-step bound came from
  22 cream achievements, overwhelmingly cream-first states, not the modal
  tomato-done failure state.

Conditioning only on `(index, kind, object)` is therefore insufficient.
Failure-state q90, `W`, `H_max`, and the “MC fallback does not fire” conclusion
are not identified for the states Action 2 would actually sample. Under the
revised criterion these derived quantities are reopened.

## 4. Scientific conclusion

V8.0 supports:

1. the evaluation harness can find two tasks with both fixed-deadline stock
   competence and headroom;
2. the two tasks expose different observed terminal signatures;
3. subgoal count alone does not predict difficulty on this ladder;
4. unordered achieved-set state and time-to-go are load-bearing variables for
   the next experiment.

V8.0 does **not** yet support:

- failure branching creates useful alternative-action coverage;
- positive corrections exist at natural anchors;
- a world model predicts or ranks sibling outcomes;
- WM-guided acquisition beats random or diversity;
- a policy update improves behavior;
- a second round moves the failure frontier;
- the observed task differences are caused by object identity alone.

Both selected tasks are nested variants in the same scene. External validity is
currently limited to this Chain-family mechanism-development setting.
