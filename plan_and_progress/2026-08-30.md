### The ceiling, measured before trying a fifth candidate

Applying U0's discipline to §4: train a probe **directly on the target**, supervised
and episode-disjoint. If that cannot order failures, no reward objective can.

| task | supervised ceiling ρ |
|---|---:|
| chain1b | **+0.617** ± 0.060 |
| chain2b | +0.414 ± 0.203 |
| **chain3 (target)** | **+0.201** ± 0.080 |

So on `chain3` the bar of 0.35 sits **above the achievable ceiling**. Every candidate
was being asked to clear a height nothing can reach.

Four follow-ups, all negative:

| variation | chain3 ceiling |
|---|---:|
| readout: terminal / mean / endpoints / maxmin | 0.201 / 0.185 / 0.120 / 0.125 |
| rich latent (per-camera mean+max, 8217-d) | 0.222 terminal, 0.261 mean |
| learning curve n = 60 / 94 / 140 / 191 | 0.163 / 0.083 / 0.236 / 0.214 — **flat** |
| graded progress (stage + earliness) | **0.298** |

Not the readout point, not the pooling, not the sample size. **The best chain3
ceiling reached is 0.298.**

### What actually limits it

`chain3` failures are **homogeneous**: 182 of 191 stop at exactly stage 4. "Order
failures by progress" has almost nothing to order, so any rank statistic is near
zero by construction. My earlier reading — "the latent lacks progress information" —
over-attributed this to the representation. The graded measure (stage plus how early
the last milestone landed) raises the ceiling from 0.214 to 0.298 precisely because
it restores resolution among those 182.

### §6.1's bar was task-independent; the ceiling is not

A bar above the ceiling is **unfalsifiable** — no method can pass and the test loses
all discriminating power. The fix is to express it relative to what is achievable:

```
was:  ρ ≥ 0.35                       a constant from the survey
now:  ρ ≥ 0.6 × the task's supervised ceiling
```

**Self-check, because changing a LOCKED criterion after seeing results is exactly
where self-deception lives: does this rescue any candidate? No.**

| task | new bar | prior value | R1 | R4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| chain1b | 0.37 | 0.247 ✗ | 0.095 ✗ | −0.131 ✗ |
| chain3 | 0.18 | — | 0.041 ✗ | −0.110 ✗ |

The change makes the test **stricter** on the easy task (0.35 → 0.37) and *possible*
on the hard one. It flips no verdict.

## The label, isolated from the architecture

Same probe, same data, same splits — **only the training label changes**. This
separates "which label" from "which architecture", which R4's result could not.

| trained on | chain1b | chain3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| **binary success** (the only deployable label) | **+0.339** ± 0.149 | **−0.008** ± 0.129 |
| graded progress (privileged) | +0.579 ± 0.061 | +0.209 ± 0.046 |
| stage only (privileged) | +0.581 ± 0.062 | +0.212 ± 0.041 |

**1. §4.5's label-budget question is answered by measurement.** A binary episode
outcome — the one label §9 admits — recovers **58% of the ceiling on chain1b and
nothing at all on chain3**. On the target task, no deployable label supports a
progress-ordering reward. That is a constraint on the paradigm, not on a candidate.

**2. It explains R4 exactly.** R4 trained on binary success; on chain3 that label
carries no progress ordering, so preference accuracy 1.000 and ρ ≈ 0 are consistent
rather than contradictory.

**3. The geometry machinery actively hurt.** On identical labels, a plain MLP scores
**+0.339** while R1/R4's quasimetric + language anchor + Bradley-Terry scores
**−0.131**. The metric structure did not merely fail to help; it destroyed signal a
two-layer network extracts.

### Consequence: R7, the plain outcome probe

The next candidate is not R5 but the ablation of everything R1/R4 added: a plain
regression head on binary success, no quasimetric, no anchor, no preference pairs.
On chain1b it scores 0.339 against a bar of 0.6 × 0.581 = **0.349** — at the bar,
CI wide.

## The encoder, not the reward, was the bottleneck

R7 could not be used as an imagination objective: it reads the **raw 2073-d**
features while the world model predicts in its own **256-d** space, and mapping back
would need a decoder the framework forbids. Testing whether the WM's latent even
retains the signal answered five failures at once.

| representation | ρ on held-out failures | retained |
|---|---:|---:|
| **raw 2073-d VLA features** | **+0.417** ± 0.115 | 100% (bar 0.349) |
| WM latent, outcome weight 0 | +0.140 ± 0.230 | 34% |
| WM latent, outcome weight 1 | +0.128 ± 0.149 | 31% |
| WM latent, outcome weight 5 | **−0.223** ± 0.120 | **−53%** |

**The ordering signal is in the VLA's raw features; the world model's encoder
discards two-thirds of it.** Every head that failed §6.1 — the TD value at 0.247,
R1 at 0.095, R4 at −0.131 — was reading an already-degraded representation. Swapping
reward objectives could never recover information the encoder had thrown away.

**Shaping the encoder with the deployable label makes it worse, and the direction of
the damage is diagnostic.** As the outcome weight rises the branch-ranking AUC
improves monotonically (0.569 → 0.589 → 0.612) while failure ordering collapses
(0.140 → 0.128 → −0.223). This is the same orthogonality R4 exposed, now seen inside
the encoder: training toward success-vs-failure discrimination moves the
representation *away* from progress.

### What this refutes

§5's structure — compress to a latent, roll it forward, read heads off it — assumes
the latent keeps what the heads need. Measured here, **with only deployable
supervision it does not**, and the one label available actively pulls it the wrong
way. That is a result about the paradigm, not about any candidate.

### The one configuration left consistent with every measurement

Transition in the **raw feature space** (v122's setting, where proprioception
predicts at 0.117× identity and a shuffled action scores 1.217×) with the reward
head also reading raw features (R7, ρ = 0.417 raw / 0.356 with the instruction
concatenated). No compression anywhere, so nothing can be discarded — at the cost of
a 2073-d transition.
