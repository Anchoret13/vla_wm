Three findings, in order of importance:

1. **Positives are real, and one is a conversion.** At `a3493`, sibling `rand1`
   beat its matched reference on all three CRN keys and turned `failure@250`
   into `success@250` on **3/3** keys where the reference succeeded **0/3**. At
   `a3494`, three siblings are paired-positive; there the gains are in
   progress/timing, not conversion — no sibling there reached success.
2. **The binding failure is the absence of negatives, not of positives.**
   Exactly **one** paired-non-improving sibling exists across all 8 anchors, so
   condition 6 scores 0/8. The pilot was designed to require both, because a
   world model needs ranking signal in both directions; what it found is
   one-sided.
3. **Five of eight anchors are completely inert** — every one of 18 comparisons
   is an exact tie at `H=80`.

### The horizon diagnostic, and what the noise floors did

| horizon | anchors with **any** readout difference | anchors clearing the registered floors |
|---|---|---|
| `H=20` | 1/8 | — |
| `H=40` | 5/8 | — |
| `H=80` | **6/8** | **3/8** |

Differences accumulate with horizon, and the sealed floors are doing real work:
6/8 anchors differ in raw readouts while only 3/8 survive the quantization
floors. Reporting the raw 6/8 as "variation" would have passed condition 4 and
turned this HALT into an ADVANCE. The floors were derived from the automaton's
10-step evaluation stride and sealed before execution, which is the only reason
that call was not available to make after the fact.

### A design defect the data exposed, for the next revision

`a3480`'s **reference succeeds on 2 of 3 CRN keys.** Its source was a genuine
`failure@250`, so it passed the registered eligibility rule — but that source
failed under *its own* continuation noise, and at the same anchor the stock
reference usually succeeds. So the anchor is not a failure anchor under the
paired protocol at all, and its single "non-improving" sibling is a sibling that
merely succeeded less often than a mostly-succeeding reference.

Eligibility was defined by the **source's** outcome; the paired comparison is
against a **freshly drawn reference**. Those are different objects. A future
stratum should require the reference itself to fail on all (or a registered
majority of) CRN keys before the anchor is admitted.

### Registered interpretation

Per §4 HALT interpretation: this anchor/proposal/setting did not expose
sufficient counterfactual support. It does **not** authorize training or
sweeping a world model, a lower bar, a larger post-outcome budget, or benchmark
shopping.

Per §5 handoff, the two options are to revise one named coverage component and
rerun a fresh prospective pilot, or to stop the formulation if supported action
diversity exists but repeatedly produces no useful outcome variation. **The
second is not indicated by this evidence**: supported diversity plainly exists
(32/32 unique pools) and it does produce useful variation at 3/8 anchors,
including one genuine failure→success conversion under full paired CRN. One
bounded revision is the indicated path; which component is a registered decision
and is not taken here.

### What this does and does not say

The action path is now clearly used — shuffling actions moves the action model's
loss and does not move the baseline's — and for the first time the action model
beats the baseline on ranking. But the improvement is small in absolute terms:
non-tie accuracy `0.0625` is **3 of 48 pairs**, far below the ~33% a
three-way guess would give, and `0.3333` macro for state-only is exactly what
predicting "tie" everywhere produces. So the correct statement is that the
action model gets a few non-ties right where the baseline gets none, not that
it ranks siblings usefully.

Against that, it is **worse on every prediction metric** — 17% higher matched
prediction loss, 2x the physical MSE, higher Brier. Adding the action path
degraded prediction while barely helping ranking.

By the registered rule this is a **second state-only result**: the indicated
move is to change the candidate proposal family, not to run another benchmark
or horizon audit, and not to sweep this architecture.

One caveat that belongs with that conclusion rather than after it: the train
split carried only 21 non-tie pairs of 512, skewed 4 better to 17 worse. The
non-tie-balanced loss put half the ranking gradient on those 21 pairs. Training
ranking signal, not just proposal diversity, may be the binding constraint — and
that is a statement about the bank, which a proposal-family change would also
address.
