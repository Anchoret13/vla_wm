### 1c. The overwrite cannot recur — `preserve_and_write`

`scripts/build_v075_hcache.py` wrote `hcache_index.json` and
`hcache_manifest.json` with a bare `write_text`, so the second invocation
(`--stage policy`) destroyed the first (`--stage lcwm`). That single line is
the whole cause of the lineage loss above.

Replaced with `preserve_and_write(path, payload, stage)`, which treats an index
as an **immutable input** once a run has trained against it:

1. identical bytes → no-op;
2. differing prior version → copied to
   `index_archive/<stem>__<sha12>.json` before anything moves;
3. new version → also frozen at
   `index_archive/<stem>__<stage>__<sha12>.json`; an existing
   content-addressed name is asserted byte-equal, never rewritten;
4. only then does the convenience pointer move, and a row lands in the
   append-only `index_lineage.jsonl`.

`.gitignore` gains `results/**/index_archive/` — the preserved copies live on
disk (they are 1.1 MB of absolute paths each, matching the existing
`hcache_index.json` exclusion), while the compact `index_lineage.jsonl` audit
trail is what gets committed.

### 1d. Recorded fidelity loss, self-inflicted, named not hidden

The builder patch in 1c was applied **before** the seal ran, and the builder
was untracked, so its run-exact bytes are unrecoverable. The seal therefore
marks `scripts/build_v075_hcache.py` with `run_exact: false` and states that
its hash binds the current repository only. The other 24 code files are
`run_exact: true`.

This is a small real loss and it is recorded rather than papered over. What the
builder produced remains bound independently by `hcache_manifest.json`
(enumeration rule, counts, input hashes) and by the surviving index hash.

### 1e. Re-sealed on the clean tree

(The seal measures worktree cleanliness before writing itself, so its own

# V7.6 registration — the bounded slice for items 2–7

Registered 2026-08-15, **before** any V7.6 collection or training. Every

## V7.6A — the pre-collection census that reshapes item 2

Item 2 says "build the next tranche for model learning, not imitation volume".
Before choosing a budget I counted what the *existing* protocol actually yields,
because the naive reading of item 2 — collect more sources — is refuted by the
count.

**V7.4B recovery yield, measured from `recovery_ledger.jsonl` (n=129):**

| | rows | positive |
|---|---|---|
| `atomic_pi05` | 66 | 3 |
| `privileged_servo` | 63 | 1 |
| **total** | **129** | **4 (3.1%)** |

and per task: t1 32/0, t2 27/2, t3 32/0, t4 6/2, t5 32/0.

The step distribution is the load-bearing observation. **127 of 129 attempts
consumed the entire 30-action budget.** The two fastest attempts took 10 and 11
steps and *both* succeeded. Recovery is bimodal: it resolves in about ten
actions or it does not resolve at all inside the frozen budget.

Three consequences, and they are what V7.6B is designed around.

1. **Scaling sources at this protocol is futile.** At 3.1% yield, tripling the
   tranche to ~90 sources buys roughly 12 grounded rows. V7.3D had **23** rows
   and still produced `lc_grounded ≈ correction_bc` at 0.00 ordered prefix.
   More imitation volume cannot reach a ledger that discriminates. This is
   exactly the failure item 2 names.
2. **The privileged servo is not the lever either.** 63 attempts, 1 positive.
   It was retained as a fallback on the assumption that scripted pick/place
   rescues what atomic π0.5 cannot; the data says it does not.
3. **The binding variable is the SNAPSHOT, not the recovery policy.** Since
   success is decided within ~10 actions, a recovery succeeds when the state it
   starts from is already within reach of the next milestone. V7.4B chose
   snapshots by where stock π0.5 happened to stall, which is precisely where
   recovery is hardest.

Item 2 already prescribes the correction — "when stock π0.5 cannot reach a
blocker, use provenance-labeled expert prefixes, successful-trajectory replay,
or acquisition-only state reachers to create the snapshot; they are
state-acquisition mechanisms, not policy targets." V7.6B promotes that from a
fallback for the empty late-blocker family to **the primary snapshot
mechanism**, on the evidence above.

## V7.6B — acquisition (item 2)

### Roles: seven, prospectively disjoint, frozen before outcome inspection

V7.4B partitioned sources three ways (`train`/`dev`/`teacher`). Item 2 requires
seven. Six are acquisition roles over source episodes and their replay
ancestors; the seventh is the behavior panel, which is seeds rather than
sources and stays disjoint from every acquisition family as it already is.

| role | sources/task | anchors/source | anchors/task |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wm_train` | 3 | 2 | 6 |
| `wm_dev` | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| `teacher_calibration` | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| `teacher_assessment` | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| `grounded_correction` | 3 | 2 | 6 |
| `policy_target` | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| **total** | **14** | | **26** |

= **70 sources / 130 acquisition anchors** across five tasks, against V7.4B's
30 / 43. `grounded_correction` gets the largest share after `wm_train` because
the grounded ledger is the measured bottleneck, not the WM.

No physical source crosses roles. Replay ancestors are partitioned with their
descendants. A seed family disjoint from every prior family and from all
behavior panels is used; the sealed/consumed/reserved panel exclusions
(`{1700..1740}`, `{1750..1790}`, `{1800..1840}`, `{1850..1890}`) carry over
unchanged.

**Language-query rotation never counts as state coverage.** Quotas below are
over unique *physical* anchors.

### Snapshot construction: four failure families, all five tasks

Item 2 names first-pick, recovery, placement, and late-chain. Per task, every
role's anchors must cover ≥ 2 distinct families, and across the tranche each
of the four families must be non-empty in **all five tasks** — the condition
V7.4B failed for LATE in 5/5.

Snapshot mechanisms, all provenance-labeled, all acquisition-only:

- `stock_stall` — where deployed π0.5 actually stalls (V7.4B's only mechanism);
- `expert_prefix` — replay a replay-valid LIBERO demo prefix to the decision
  preceding the target blocker, then hand control to π0.5. The
  `demo_rehearsal_v067` bank (≈10 train + 2 dev demos/task, replay-validated)
  is the source;
- `success_replay` — replay a previously successful trajectory's prefix;
- `state_reacher` — the privileged scripted servo used *to reach a snapshot*
  rather than to recover from one.

**Hard rule, registered:** a prefix mechanism creates the *state*. It is never
a policy target, never enters any ledger as a supervised action, and every row
it produces carries `snapshot_mechanism` so that a later analysis can exclude
it. Recovery and sibling actions are executed by policy-supported samples only.

### Recovery protocol, two registered changes

1. **Budget 30 → 60 actions / ≤ 6 decisions.** Weak prior: 2 of 4 V7.4B
   positives landed exactly at the 30-action wall, so some attempts were
   truncated rather than failed. Registered as an *exploratory widening*; if
   the extra 30 actions add no positives, that is reported as evidence the
   bimodality is real and the budget returns to 30.
2. **Privileged servo demoted from recovery to snapshot construction.** On
   1/63, it is not retained as a recovery instrument. It stays as a
   `state_reacher`, where its determinism is what is wanted.

### Registered support quotas, bound BEFORE collection

Per task, over unique physical anchors:

| quota | minimum |
|---|---|
| positive recovery rows | ≥ 4 |
| positive recovery rows carried to terminal | ≥ 2 |
| pos + matched-nonpos sibling pairs | ≥ 3 |
| distinct failure families represented | ≥ 3 of 4 |
| `grounded_correction` anchors yielding ≥ 1 positive | ≥ 3 |

Tranche-wide: **≥ 25 positive recovery rows over ≥ 15 unique anchors and all 5
tasks** — i.e. the grounded ledger must exceed V7.3D's 23 rows / 6 anchors /
5 tasks, the configuration already shown to be insufficient. Anything at or
below V7.3D is a *failed acquisition*, not a thin success, and routes to the
halt clause below.

### Registered halt clause — the thing V7.5 lacked

V7.5's Stage 4 census found 4 grounded rows and had no pre-registered rule to

## V7.6C — one action-identifying LCWM (item 3)

Architecture: V7.5 `hist_center + r_norm`, unchanged and now committed at
`1f28a54`. Objective adds, per item 3:

- matched **wrong-action** and **shuffled-action** rollout controls, so scene
  or phase alone cannot satisfy the rollout term — the term V7.5 trained to
  +1e-6;
- **same-state sibling outcome** losses over the paired candidates V7.6B
  collects.

Per-epoch, on source-held-out data: raw `hist_rel`, rollout margin,
action-effect and sibling discrimination vs copy / no-action / stock, physical
effects, goal-conditioned progress, paraphrase invariance, action × goal
reversals. Each names its support; empty support prints `unsupported`.

### Checkpoint selection, prospective and joint

V7.5 selected the final step and got a collapsed recurrence. The Stage 3
process lesson was that a threshold anchored to a *dead* baseline is vacuous.
So both conditions are anchored to demonstrated-live values:

1. **history retention** `hist_rel ≥ 0.10` — 42% of V7.5's demonstrated-live
   plateau of 0.237, an externally demonstrated value, *not* a multiple of
   V7.5's dead ~1e-6 and *not* a fraction of this run's own maximum (which
   would be data-seen within the run);
2. **action-effect discrimination** — beats the copy baseline on **≥ 25%** of
   source-held-out rows, by more than the replay-noise margin. V7.5 scored
   0/72; the V7.4B `replay_fidelity` audit puts state replay noise at
   `obj_pos = 0.0`, `qpos = 0.0`, `eef_pos ≤ 2.2e-3`, so the margin is set at
   `5e-3` in eef units.

The **earliest** epoch satisfying both is frozen. If no epoch satisfies both,
**no checkpoint is promoted** and V7.6D–G do not run; V7.6 terminates as a
registered model-bounded negative. Final epoch, aggregate loss, task identity,
and any centered distance inflated by small `sigma` are explicitly barred from
selecting the model.
